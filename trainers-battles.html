Trainer, Capture & Battle Systems

Trainer Status

Anyone can become a Trainer. There is no special aptitude, bloodline, profession, or other inherent requirement needed to become one. Being a Trainer is a normal and common part of society. Most people have at least one Entheon, although being a Trainer does not necessarily mean that someone is a competitive or professional battler. A Trainer may have Entheon for companionship, work, recreation, battling, research, protection, transportation, or other practical purposes.

Age Requirement

The minimum age for independent Trainer status is 15. At age 15, a person may:

  • Register as a Trainer.
  • Receive a TraID.
  • Legally own Entheon.
  • Purchase and register Capture Crystals.
  • Capture Entheon independently.
  • Participate in official Trainer battles.
  • Challenge Gyms.
  • Enter tournaments and other Trainer competitions.

People under 15 may still live with Entheon, care for them, train alongside them, and form bonds with them under the responsibility of a parent, guardian, or other legally responsible adult. There is no separate junior TraID or junior Trainer licensing system.

15 is the age of full independent Trainer status.

Trainer Registration

Anyone who captures their first Entheon must legally register as a Trainer. Registration is completed through an authorized Trainer registration facility. Upon registration, the Trainer receives a TraID, which serves as their official Trainer identification. Registration is straightforward and widely accessible. There is no restrictive licensing process or test determining whether someone is considered worthy of becoming a Trainer.

Capture Crystal Registration

Capture Crystals are individually identifiable and are linked to their legal owner. When a Trainer purchases a new Capture Crystal, they present their TraID. The crystal is then automatically registered and linked to that Trainer, even while it remains empty. When an Entheon is captured into a registered Capture Crystal, the crystal automatically identifies the Entheon’s unique biological signature. The Entheon is then associated with that Capture Crystal and the Trainer’s TraID. No separate ownership filing is required for each individual capture. The basic process is:

Purchase Crystal → Present TraID → Crystal linked to Trainer → Capture Entheon → Biological signature recognized → Entheon registered to Trainer

This registration system also provides the foundation for ownership transfers, trading, stolen Entheon, and illegal captures.

Battle Culture

Entheon battles are a common and socially accepted part of everyday life. Battles occur in many different contexts, including:

  • Roadside Trainer battles.
  • Friendly and practice battles.
  • Gym challenges.
  • Local events.
  • Tournaments.
  • Professional competitions.
  • Occupational and practical training.

Battling is not restricted to professional Trainers. Ordinary Trainers may battle for recreation, practice, social interaction, reputation, or income.

Roadside Trainer Battles

Registered Trainers may challenge one another while travelling. Roadside battles can provide legitimate income, making travelling and battling a viable lifestyle for some Trainers. Depending on the circumstances, battles may involve agreed rewards such as money, items, services, or other forms of compensation. A Trainer is not required to accept every challenge. Declining a battle is not inherently suspicious or dishonorable. Because Entheon ownership and battling are so common, encountering Trainers battling in appropriate public areas is a normal part of everyday life.

Battle Safety

A fundamental rule of Entheon combat is:

An Entheon cannot kill another Entheon through direct combat.

Entheon battles can still result in:

  • A knockout.
  • Exhaustion.
  • Temporary injuries.
  • Serious but non-lethal injuries.
  • The need for medical treatment or recovery.

However, direct Entheon-on-Entheon combat cannot result in the death of an Entheon. This is an inherent rule of Entheon combat rather than merely a regulation imposed by competitive organizations. This does not mean that Entheon are immortal. Entheon can still die from natural causes, illness, accidents, environmental hazards, or other circumstances unrelated to direct Entheon combat. The rule also reinforces the individual agency of Entheon. A Trainer cannot simply command an Entheon to kill another Entheon.

Gyms

There are seven recognized Gyms. Each Gym:

  • Specializes in a fixed Affinity.
  • Has a designated Gym Leader.
  • Can be challenged in any order.
  • Has an informal recommended progression based on expected difficulty.
  • Does not require previous Gyms to be completed before it can be challenged.

The recommended order is guidance rather than a mandatory progression system. A sufficiently experienced Trainer may challenge a later or more difficult Gym without first completing the others.

Gym Crests

Defeating a Gym Leader awards the Trainer that Gym’s Crest. There are seven Gym Crests corresponding to the seven recognized Gyms. Crests are physical achievements and insignia representing a Trainer’s successful Gym challenges. They may be collected in any order. The exact significance of possessing all seven Gym Crests, including what competitions or opportunities they qualify a Trainer for, will be established as part of the wider League and tournament structure.

Professional and Competitive Battling

Battling exists across a broad spectrum. A Trainer may battle casually with friends, challenge other Trainers while travelling, participate in local events, pursue Gym challenges, or eventually compete at a professional level. Professional battling can therefore become a legitimate career, although the exact structure of professional competition, rankings, sponsorships, tournaments, and leagues is defined separately.

Rogue Trainers

Because Trainer registration is legally required after capturing an Entheon, unregistered Trainers can exist outside the established system. A rogue Trainer is an individual who possesses or trains captured Entheon without maintaining the legally required Trainer registration. Being a rogue Trainer does not automatically mean that the individual is a criminal. However, registration is straightforward, accessible, and extremely common, meaning that most people who deliberately remain unregistered are expected to have a reason for avoiding the legal system. Rogue Trainers may be associated with activities such as:

  • Stolen Entheon.
  • Illegal Entheon trafficking.
  • Underground battling.
  • Concealing their identity.
  • Criminal organizations.
  • Deliberately avoiding legal restrictions or oversight.

Rogue Trainer is primarily a common social term rather than necessarily a formal legal classification. The exact enforcement mechanisms and legal consequences surrounding rogue Trainers will be established alongside the broader rules for Entheon ownership, capture, transfer, and theft.

3. Entheon Ownership & Capture

Legal Ownership

An Entheon becomes legally associated with a Trainer when it is successfully captured into a Capture Crystal registered to that Trainer. Capture therefore establishes both the physical containment of the Entheon and its legal ownership record. The Capture Crystal’s registration system automatically links the captured Entheon to the Trainer’s TraID.

Capturing Wild Entheon

Wild Entheon can be captured using Capture Crystals. A successful capture places the Entheon within the Capture Crystal and registers its unique biological signature to the crystal and its Trainer. Capture does not automatically determine an Entheon’s personality, Nature, or Bond with its Trainer. An Entheon remains an individual being with its own personality, preferences, memories, and agency after capture. A newly captured Entheon may therefore require time and interaction to become comfortable with its new Trainer.

Capture Crystal Habitats

A Capture Crystal is more than a container. When an Entheon is captured, the crystal creates and maintains a self-contained miniature habitat suited to that individual Entheon’s needs. The habitat automatically adapts to the Entheon’s biological and environmental requirements. An Entheon’s habitat is influenced by factors such as:

  • Species.
  • Affinity.
  • Biological requirements.
  • Environmental preferences.
  • Individual characteristics.

Affinity alone does not determine the habitat. Two Entheon with the same Affinity may therefore have completely different environments within their Capture Crystals. For example, one Fire-affinity Entheon may thrive in a volcanic environment containing heated rock and magma, while another Fire-affinity Entheon may prefer a warm, densely vegetated forest environment. The Capture Crystal provides a safe, self-sustaining environment in which the Entheon can rest, sleep, eat, and otherwise live comfortably while contained.

Habitat Adaptation

A Capture Crystal’s internal habitat can change as its Entheon changes. Evolution, changes in Affinity, maturation, or changing biological requirements may cause the habitat to adapt accordingly. The habitat therefore represents the individual Entheon, rather than simply providing a generic environment based on its species or Affinity.

Capture Does Not Equal Obedience

Capturing an Entheon establishes legal ownership and provides a safe habitat, but it does not erase the Entheon’s individuality or automatically create complete obedience. An Entheon retains:

  • Its Nature.
  • Its Personality.
  • Its memories.
  • Its preferences.
  • Its individual agency.
  • Its existing relationships and experiences.

The quality of the relationship between Trainer and Entheon is instead influenced by their interactions and Bond. A newly captured Entheon may therefore be cooperative, uncertain, resistant, enthusiastic, frightened, curious, or otherwise behave according to its individual Personality and circumstances.

Capture establishes ownership. It does not manufacture loyalty.

Transfer of Ownership

Entheon can be legally transferred between Trainers through mechanisms such as trading or gifting. When an Entheon is transferred, its registration is updated to reflect the new Trainer and Capture Crystal ownership. A transfer does not erase the Entheon’s:

  • Memories.
  • Nature.
  • Personality.
  • Previous experiences.
  • Existing Bond history.

Legal ownership and emotional attachment are therefore separate concepts. An Entheon may legally belong to a new Trainer while still remembering and caring about its previous Trainer.

Stolen Entheon

Taking another Trainer’s Entheon or Capture Crystal without authorization is illegal. Because Capture Crystals are individually registered, simply taking a crystal does not automatically transfer ownership. The existing registration remains associated with the original Trainer until a legitimate ownership transfer is completed. The exact legal procedures surrounding stolen Entheon, ownership disputes, illegal captures, and rogue Trainers will be established as part of the wider ownership and enforcement system.

4. Entheon Storage

Active Entheon Limit

A Trainer may have up to six Entheon in their active group at any given time. Six is the maximum number of Entheon that a Trainer normally carries with them while travelling, battling, or participating in activities. There is no limit to the total number of Entheon a Trainer may legally own. A Trainer may therefore capture and own additional Entheon beyond their six active Entheon.

Automatic Storage

When a Trainer captures an Entheon while already having six active Entheon, the newly captured Entheon is automatically transferred to the Trainer’s registered remote storage. The Trainer does not lose ownership of the Entheon. It simply becomes part of their stored collection rather than their active group. The basic process is:

Fewer than six active Entheon → newly captured Entheon joins the active group.

Six active Entheon → newly captured Entheon is automatically transferred to registered storage.

A Trainer may later exchange an active Entheon with one held in storage. Storage transfers automatically update the Trainer’s registered Entheon roster.

Remote Storage

Entheon that are not part of a Trainer’s active group are maintained in secure remote storage. Stored Entheon continue to live within their Capture Crystals’ individual habitats and remain legally associated with their Trainer. A Trainer can retrieve an Entheon from storage and exchange it with one of their active Entheon. The exact technical infrastructure used to access and operate the remote storage network will be established as part of the world’s broader technology rules.

Entheon Activity Requirement

Entheon are living beings rather than permanent collectibles. A Trainer is expected to maintain an ongoing relationship with their Entheon, including those held in storage. If a Trainer has no meaningful interaction with an Entheon for more than one month, the storage system flags that Entheon as inactive. The Trainer receives a notification or warning before the inactivity period expires. If the Trainer does not meaningfully interact with the Entheon before the period expires, the Entheon is automatically released into the wild. When this occurs:

  • The Entheon’s ownership registration is terminated.
  • The Entheon is no longer associated with the Trainer’s TraID.
  • The Capture Crystal is returned to the Trainer as an empty, unregistered crystal.
  • The released Entheon becomes independent and is free to establish its own life in the wild.

Meaningful Interaction

Meaningful interaction can include:

  • Taking the Entheon out of storage.
  • Spending time with the Entheon.
  • Training with it.
  • Battling with it.
  • Travelling with it.
  • Caring for it.
  • Personally checking on or interacting with it through an approved storage system.
  • Other direct interaction that demonstrates an ongoing relationship.

Simply owning the Entheon, keeping its Capture Crystal registered, or viewing its record does not reset the inactivity period.

Long-Term Inactive Status

A Trainer may place an Entheon into an approved long-term inactive status when there is a legitimate reason for extended separation. While in this status, the normal one-month inactivity release does not apply. This allows Trainers to maintain ownership of Entheon that temporarily cannot travel with them or receive regular interaction without risking automatic release.

Ownership of an Entheon carries an ongoing responsibility toward that individual.

Active Group & Storage

The six-Entheon active limit is a travel and activity limit, not an ownership limit. A Trainer may therefore maintain a larger collection of Entheon while only travelling with six at a time. This allows Trainers to rotate their active Entheon, develop relationships with different individuals, and select teams appropriate to different situations without treating additional Entheon as disposable possessions.

5. Entheon Battles

General Battle Culture

Entheon battles are a common and socially accepted part of everyday life. Battles can take place in many different contexts, including:

  • Casual battles between friends or acquaintances.
  • Roadside Trainer battles.
  • Gym challenges.
  • Local events and festivals.
  • Exhibition and mock battles.
  • Tournaments.
  • Professional competitions.
  • Training and practical exercises.

The number of Entheon used in an ordinary battle is not universally fixed. Trainers may agree on an appropriate battle format before the battle begins. Common formats can include:

  • 1v1
  • 2v2
  • 3v3
  • 4v4
  • 6v6

Official competitions and Gyms may use their own established formats.

Battle Commands & Entheon Agency

Trainers issue commands to their active Entheon during battle. Commands are instructions rather than absolute control. Entheon retain their individual agency and interpret commands according to their:

  • Nature.
  • Personality.
  • Training.
  • Experience.
  • Bond with their Trainer.
  • Current physical and emotional condition.

An Entheon may therefore refuse, ignore, reinterpret, or otherwise react differently to a command when circumstances warrant it. A strong Trainer is not necessarily one whose Entheon obeys every command without question. Effective battling depends on the ability of a Trainer and their Entheon to understand and coordinate with one another.

Battle Termination

A battle normally ends when:

  • All participating Entheon on one side have been knocked out.
  • A Trainer forfeits the battle.
  • An official or referee stops the battle for safety.
  • Another agreed victory condition is reached.

A knocked-out Entheon cannot continue fighting until it has recovered sufficiently. The exact recovery time depends on the Entheon, the severity of the battle, and the circumstances.

Switching Entheon

Trainers may switch their active Entheon during battle unless a particular battle format or event establishes different rules. Switching allows Trainers to adapt their strategy to changing battle conditions and make use of different Entheon within their active group. An Entheon that is switched out remains part of the battle unless it has been knocked out or otherwise removed from participation.

Move Usage & Stamina

Entheon do not have an artificial limit on how many times a particular move or ability can be used. Instead, repeated use is naturally limited by factors such as:

  • Stamina.
  • Physical strain.
  • Energy expenditure.
  • Concentration.
  • The nature of the move or ability.
  • The Entheon’s physical condition.
  • The surrounding environment where relevant.

A powerful move may therefore be technically usable multiple times, but repeatedly using it can exhaust or otherwise strain the Entheon. Battle performance is consequently influenced by resource management and tactical decision-making, rather than an arbitrary number of permitted uses.

Battle Safety

A fundamental rule of Entheon combat is:

An Entheon cannot kill another Entheon through direct combat.

Entheon battles can still result in:

  • Knockouts.
  • Exhaustion.
  • Temporary injuries.
  • Serious but non-lethal injuries.
  • The need for medical treatment or recovery.

However, direct Entheon-on-Entheon combat cannot result in the death of an Entheon. This is an inherent rule of Entheon combat rather than merely a regulation imposed by competitive organizations. Entheon can still die from natural causes, illness, accidents, environmental hazards, or other circumstances unrelated to direct Entheon combat.

Gym Battles

There are seven recognized Gyms, each specializing in a fixed Affinity. Gym battles use established formats based on the Gym’s recommended difficulty.

Gym Recommended Difficulty Battle Format
Gym 1 Introductory 3v3
Gym 2 Beginner 3v3
Gym 3 Intermediate 3v3
Gym 4 Intermediate/Advanced 4v4
Gym 5 Advanced 4v4
Gym 6 Expert 6v6
Gym 7 Master 6v6

The Gyms may be challenged in any order. The recommended progression represents increasing expected difficulty rather than a mandatory sequence. A Trainer who chooses to challenge a more difficult Gym early must participate in that Gym’s established battle format.

Gym Leader Rosters

Every Gym Leader has a defined roster of six Entheon. The six-member roster represents the Gym Leader’s established team, battle philosophy, and Affinity specialization. The roster remains fixed unless the story or setting establishes that the Gym Leader has changed their team.

Gyms 1–5

For Gyms using fewer than six Entheon, the Gym Leader selects the required number of Entheon from their established six-member roster. Gym battle rosters are selected for an individual challenger based on relevant circumstances, including:

  • The challenger’s experience.
  • The challenger’s demonstrated strategy.
  • The Entheon’s strengths and weaknesses.
  • Battle compatibility.
  • The intended difficulty of the challenge.
  • The Gym Leader’s personality and battle philosophy.

The Gym Leader’s selection is not revealed to the challenger beforehand.

Gyms 6–7

Gyms 6 and 7 use the Gym Leader’s complete six-Entheon roster.

Local Battle Events

Entheon battles are also incorporated into local culture and community celebrations. Towns and cities may feature:

  • Festival battles.
  • Exhibition matches.
  • Mock Trainer battles.
  • Demonstrations.
  • Community tournaments.
  • Charity battles.
  • Seasonal competitions.

These events may take place during celebrations such as lunar festivals, harvest festivals, city anniversaries, or other local traditions. Not every battle at such an event is intended as serious competition. Some are primarily entertainment, allowing spectators to enjoy skilled Trainers and Entheon performing in a festive setting. Entheon battling is therefore naturally integrated into everyday culture rather than existing solely as a professional competitive activity.

Types of Battles

The world contains several broad forms of Entheon battling:

Casual Battles

Informal battles between Trainers for recreation, practice, social interaction, or friendly competition.

Roadside Battles

Trainer challenges encountered while travelling. These may involve agreed rewards or wagers and can provide legitimate income for travelling Trainers.

Gym Battles

Formal challenges against recognized Gym Leaders, using the established format of each Gym and awarding a Gym Crest upon victory.

Local Events

Community-focused battles, exhibitions, demonstrations, and competitions associated with festivals and other local celebrations.

Tournaments

Organized competitive events in which Trainers compete against one another under established rules.

Professional Battles

High-level competitive battling that can form part or all of a Trainer’s career. The exact structure of professional leagues, rankings, sponsorships, and major tournaments is established separately as part of the wider competitive system.
