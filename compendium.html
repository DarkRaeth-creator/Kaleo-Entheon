Species Entry Framework

Each species entry is structured around the following core categories:

  1. Species concept — core identity and archetype.
  2. Anatomy & appearance — body plan, proportions, head, limbs, wings/tail and distinctive anatomy.
  3. Surface & coloration — material/texture, primary colours, secondary colours and markings.
  4. Affinity manifestation — how each affinity appears biologically, visually and functionally.
  5. Personality & behaviour — temperament, habits and ecological behaviour.
  6. Combat identity — broad fighting style and characteristic affinity use.
  7. Evolution progression — how each stage develops from the previous one.
  8. Silhouette priority — the most important visual feature for recognition.

Not every species requires every category to be stated separately; descriptions prioritize the information most relevant to that species.

Global design rules

  • These are original species, not renamed copies of existing Pokémon.
  • Existing creatures may inspire broad archetypes or body plans, but each final design needs its own identity.
  • Evolution stages should remain recognizably related while becoming meaningfully more developed.
  • Normal three-stage lines retain their established affinities from stage 2 into stage 3.
  • Branching evolution may intentionally replace the base affinity.
  • Affinity should influence anatomy, behaviour and abilities without reducing every species to an elemental cliché.
  • Common species may be simple; rare and Legendary species may be more elaborate.
  • Legendary status does not automatically mean enormous size.
  • No creature should accidentally duplicate another creature’s primary silhouette or ecological niche.
  • Written descriptions establish the intended canonical species identity, anatomy, behaviour and Affinity expression.
