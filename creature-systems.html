Creature Combat System

1. Purpose

The Creature Combat System defines how creatures battle, develop, learn techniques, use innate abilities, and interact with Affinities. The system is intentionally structured similarly to established creature-battling RPG systems. It uses familiar concepts such as creature statistics, elemental affinities, Physical/Special/Status techniques, abilities, learnsets, type effectiveness, status conditions, and evolutionary progression.

2. Creature Data

Every creature species has the following core combat information: - Species Name - Affinity - Rarity - Evolution Stage - Evolution Line - Base Statistics - Innate Abilities - Signature Moves - Level-Up Moves - Technique/TM Compatibility - Move Compatibility Restrictions - Evolution-Related Moves or Abilities A creature’s combat capabilities should be derived from its established species design, Affinity, anatomy, evolution and documented abilities. A creature cannot use abilities, moves or powers that contradict its established species data.

3. Creature Statistics

Every creature possesses six primary combat statistics:

HP — Health Points

Determines how much damage a creature can sustain before it is unable to continue battling.

Attack

Determines the effectiveness of primarily Physical attacks.

Defense

Determines the creature’s ability to withstand primarily Physical attacks.

Special Attack

Determines the effectiveness of primarily Special attacks.

Special Defense

Determines the creature’s ability to withstand primarily Special attacks.

Speed

Determines how quickly the creature can act in battle and influences turn order.

4. Combat Categories

Every damaging technique belongs to one of two primary damage categories.

Physical

Physical techniques represent attacks relying primarily on the creature’s physical body, strength, claws, teeth, limbs, wings, weapons, or other direct physical mechanisms. Physical damage is primarily determined by:

Attacker’s Attack → Defender’s Defense

Examples include: - Bites - Claw attacks - Tackles - Wing strikes - Tail strikes - Physical Flame attacks - Physical Volt attacks

Special

Special techniques represent attacks relying primarily on Affinity energy, ranged projection, supernatural abilities, elemental manipulation, psychic effects, or other non-physical mechanisms. Special damage is primarily determined by:

Attacker’s Special Attack → Defender’s Special Defense

Examples include: - Flame projectiles - Lightning attacks - Water techniques - Psychic/Mystic techniques - Radiant beams - Umbral energy attacks A technique’s Affinity does not determine whether it is Physical or Special. Both Physical and Special techniques can exist within the same Affinity.

5. Status Techniques

Status techniques do not primarily deal direct damage. Instead, they alter the battle through effects such as: - Increasing or decreasing statistics - Inflicting status conditions - Healing - Protecting the user - Altering the battlefield - Altering weather - Creating Affinity-based fields - Applying temporary advantages or disadvantages - Manipulating turn order - Restricting or disrupting opposing creatures Status techniques may belong to an Affinity or may be Neutral/General techniques.

6. Technique Data

Every technique has a defined data entry containing: - Name - Affinity - Category - Power - Accuracy - PP - Priority - Effect - Target - Learn Method - Species Compatibility, where applicable

Power

Determines the base strength of a damaging technique. Status techniques normally have no Power value.

Accuracy

Determines the probability that a technique successfully affects its intended target. Certain techniques may intentionally have imperfect accuracy in exchange for greater power or stronger effects.

PP

Determines how many times a technique can normally be used before requiring recovery or restoration.

Priority

Determines whether a technique can act before or after normal turn order. Most techniques have normal priority. Certain techniques may have increased or decreased priority as part of their design.

7. Affinities

Every creature possesses one or two Affinities. The established Affinities are: 1. Flame 2. Tide 3. Verdant 4. Gale 5. Volt 6. Frost 7. Stone 8. Metal 9. Mystic 10. Radiant 11. Umbral 12. Wild 13. Dragon A creature’s Affinity represents an inherent aspect of its biology, energy, abilities and combat identity. A creature’s Affinity should influence its available techniques and abilities, but should not completely determine its visual appearance, personality or combat style.

8. Single-Affinity Creatures

A single-Affinity creature possesses one Affinity. Example:

Quiblet

Affinity: Flame Quiblet receives the benefits associated with Flame and has access to appropriate Flame techniques. Its anatomy and established species design determine which Physical, Status and General techniques it can additionally learn.

9. Dual-Affinity Creatures

A dual-Affinity creature possesses two Affinities. Example:

Quivane

Affinity: Flame / Volt A dual-Affinity creature may use techniques associated with either of its Affinities and receives the same-affinity benefit when using an appropriate technique. Dual-Affinity creatures do not automatically gain every technique belonging to either Affinity. Technique compatibility remains dependent upon species, anatomy and established learnsets.

10. Same-Affinity Technique Bonus

A creature receives a Same-Affinity Bonus when using a technique that matches one of its own Affinities. For a single-Affinity creature: > Creature: Flame >  Technique: Flame >  Result: Same-Affinity Bonus For a dual-Affinity creature: > Creature: Flame / Volt >  Technique: Flame >  Result: Same-Affinity Bonus > Creature: Flame / Volt >  Technique: Volt >  Result: Same-Affinity Bonus > Creature: Flame / Volt >  Technique: Tide >  Result: No Same-Affinity Bonus The exact numerical value of the Same-Affinity Bonus will be established separately.

11. Affinity Effectiveness

Affinities interact with one another during combat. Every Affinity matchup will eventually be assigned an effectiveness value. The planned categories are: - Super Effective - Effective - Neutral - Resisted - Highly Resisted - Immune, where appropriate The complete Affinity interaction chart will be established separately. Battles use the documented Affinity interaction rules rather than inventing effectiveness relationships during individual battles.

12. Abilities

Abilities represent innate characteristics that affect how a creature behaves or performs in battle. An ability may be: - Passive - Triggered by a specific condition - Triggered when entering battle - Triggered when the creature’s HP reaches a threshold - Triggered by a particular Affinity interaction - Triggered by a specific battlefield condition - Activated through a specific combat event Each species should normally possess one or two standard abilities. Some species may additionally possess a Hidden Ability. Abilities should reinforce the established species identity rather than function as arbitrary additional powers.

13. Signature Moves

Every species may possess one to three Signature Moves. Signature Moves are techniques strongly associated with a particular species and are not intended to be interchangeable with generic Affinity techniques. A Signature Move should reflect at least one of the following: - The creature’s anatomy - Its Affinity - Its personality - Its ecological behaviour - Its evolutionary identity - Its distinctive combat style Example:

Sindra

Signature Move: Shadowflame Rush   Affinity: Flame / Umbral   Category: Physical The exact mechanics of individual Signature Moves will be established within the species’ own data entry.

14. General Affinity Move Pools

Each Affinity possesses a shared pool of General Techniques. These techniques represent commonly occurring techniques associated with that Affinity. For example, the Flame Move Pool may contain: - Basic Flame techniques - Physical Flame techniques - Special Flame techniques - Flame Status techniques - Defensive Flame techniques - Utility Flame techniques Species may learn appropriate techniques from their Affinity pools according to their anatomy, evolution, rarity and individual learnset. Possessing an Affinity does not automatically grant access to every technique within that Affinity’s move pool.

15. General/Neutral Move Pool

A separate General Move Pool exists for techniques that are not intrinsically tied to a specific Affinity. Examples may include: - Basic physical attacks - Defensive techniques - Movement techniques - Recovery techniques - Focus techniques - Utility techniques Compatibility depends on species anatomy and established learnsets. For example, an avian creature may learn appropriate Wing-based techniques, while a creature without wings cannot.

16. Anatomical Compatibility

A creature’s physical anatomy determines which techniques it can reasonably learn. Examples: - Creatures with wings may learn appropriate wing or aerial techniques. - Creatures with claws may learn claw-based techniques. - Creatures with fangs may learn bite-based techniques. - Serpentine creatures may learn constriction or coil-based techniques. - Aquatic creatures may possess techniques requiring water-based movement. - Creatures without limbs cannot arbitrarily learn techniques requiring conventional limbs. An Entheon cannot use an anatomically impossible technique unless its species documentation explicitly establishes the necessary ability.

17. Evolution and Techniques

Evolution represents progression rather than replacement. When a creature evolves, it normally retains previously learned techniques unless an established species mechanic specifically states otherwise. An evolved creature may: - Retain previous techniques - Learn stronger versions of existing techniques - Gain new techniques - Gain access to additional Affinity techniques - Gain new Signature Moves - Develop new abilities - Replace or modify certain abilities where explicitly documented Evolution should produce a recognizable progression from the previous stage rather than creating an unrelated moveset.

18. Evolution Affinity Rules

For standard three-stage evolutionary lines, the established Affinity progression must be respected. A creature may acquire a second Affinity during evolution. Once a second Affinity has been acquired, it remains consistent through subsequent stages unless the species is explicitly documented as having a special branching or replacement evolution mechanic. Example: > Stage 1: Flame >  Stage 2: Flame / Volt >  Stage 3: Flame / Volt The Stage 3 creature does not arbitrarily change to a different Affinity. Special evolutionary lines may intentionally use different rules when explicitly documented.

19. Branching Evolution

Certain species may possess multiple possible evolutionary forms. A branching evolutionary species may replace or alter its original Affinity depending on the evolutionary path. This is an intentional exception to the standard evolutionary Affinity rule. The individual branch must explicitly document: - Evolution condition - Resulting species - Resulting Affinity - New abilities - New Signature Moves - Changes to the learnset The Eevee-style concept established for one two-stage line is an example of a species where the base Affinity may be replaced by the chosen evolution.

20. Move Acquisition

Techniques may be acquired through several methods.

Starting Moves

Techniques available when a creature first becomes battle-ready.

Level-Up Moves

Techniques learned naturally as the creature grows stronger.

Evolution Moves

Techniques gained upon reaching a new evolutionary stage.

Signature Moves

Species-specific techniques associated with the creature’s unique identity.

Technique/TM Compatibility

Techniques that can be deliberately taught to compatible creatures.

Special Techniques

Techniques obtained through unusual circumstances, training, story events or other documented methods.

21. Learnset Philosophy

A creature’s complete learnset should reflect its identity. The learnset should consider: - Affinity - Anatomy - Evolution - Rarity - Personality - Ecological role - Established combat identity A creature should not receive a technique solely because that technique exists within one of its Affinities. Likewise, two creatures sharing an Affinity should not necessarily have identical movesets. Their Signature Moves and learnsets should provide meaningful differentiation.

22. Status Conditions

The system supports major and minor combat conditions.

Major Conditions

  • Burn
  • Poison
  • Paralysis
  • Sleep
  • Freeze

Secondary Conditions and Effects

  • Confusion
  • Flinching
  • Accuracy reduction
  • Evasion reduction
  • Attack reduction
  • Defense reduction
  • Special Attack reduction
  • Special Defense reduction
  • Speed reduction
  • Other temporary combat effects The exact rules and durations for each condition will be defined separately.

23. Battlefield Effects

Certain techniques and abilities may alter the battlefield. Possible battlefield mechanics include:

Weather

Examples: - Rain - Sun - Snow - Storm

Affinity Fields

Examples: - Radiant Field - Umbral Field - Verdant Field - Mystic Field Battlefield effects may: - Strengthen specific Affinity techniques - Weaken specific Affinity techniques - Alter creature abilities - Alter move effects - Apply environmental conditions - Change strategic priorities The complete battlefield system will be defined separately.
