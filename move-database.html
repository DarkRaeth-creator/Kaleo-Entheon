Move Database

Purpose

This document establishes the reusable move pool for the 13 creature Affinities and the General/Neutral pool. These moves form the shared mechanical vocabulary from which individual species learnsets will be constructed. This version deliberately treats moves as a complete combat toolkit, rather than as an attack catalogue. Offensive attacks, Status moves, utility, defensive techniques, recovery, cleansing, setup and field interaction are all represented. Species-specific Signature Moves are intentionally excluded and will be established separately.

Move Data Fields

Every move entry contains: - Move Name - Affinity - Category - Power - Accuracy - PP - Priority - Target - Effect

Category

  • Physical — uses the user’s physical offensive capability.
  • Special — uses the user’s special offensive capability.
  • Status — does not directly deal damage and instead produces a condition, buff, debuff, recovery effect, field effect or other utility.

Power

The base offensive strength of a damaging move. Status moves have no Power value.

Accuracy

The base probability that a move successfully affects its intended target.

PP

The normal number of times the move may be used before requiring restoration.

Priority

Determines whether a move acts before or after normal turn order.

Target

Defines the intended target pattern, such as: - Single opponent - All opponents - User - Ally - User or ally - Allied side - Field - All combatants

Move Pool Design Philosophy

The Affinity pools are toolkits, not lists of attacks. Individual Affinities should have recognizable mechanical identities. A typical Affinity pool should contain a mixture of: - Basic offensive moves - Stronger offensive moves - Physical and/or Special options where appropriate - Status moves - Buffs and debuffs - Defensive or utility techniques - Recovery or sustain where thematically appropriate - Field/weather interaction where appropriate - One or more distinctive mechanical effects Not every Affinity needs access to direct HP recovery. In particular: - Verdant specializes in natural regeneration and vitality. - Radiant specializes in restoration, purification and protective light. - Mystic specializes in arcane/psychic restoration and mental resilience. - Other Affinities can specialize in defense, speed, disruption, offense, battlefield control or other identities instead of receiving generic healing. The exact number of moves in each pool may be expanded later.

Recovery Philosophy

Recovery should not be treated as a mandatory feature of every Affinity. Instead, recovery should help establish mechanical identity.

Verdant — Regeneration

Verdant recovery represents natural life force, growth and biological renewal. Verdant recovery can: - Restore HP directly - Restore HP over multiple turns - Become stronger under Verdant field effects - Interact with draining or plant-based techniques

Radiant — Restoration and Purification

Radiant recovery represents cleansing, healing light and restoration. Radiant recovery can: - Restore HP - Remove negative status conditions - Protect against further negative effects - Support allies - Interact with Radiant field effects

Mystic — Arcane Restoration

Mystic recovery represents psychic focus, magical energy and mental resilience. Mystic recovery can: - Restore HP - Strengthen Special Defense - Protect against mental/status disruption - Restore allies through controlled energy - Interact with Mystic field effects

General — Basic Recovery

General recovery provides universally understandable survival tools without giving a creature a specialized Affinity identity. Recover, Rest, Endure and similar moves belong here.

Other Affinities

Other Affinities are not required to possess direct healing. They can instead sustain themselves through: - Damage mitigation - Defensive buffs - Drain effects - Speed and evasion - Field control - Status disruption - Recoil tradeoffs - Offensive pressure This prevents every Affinity from becoming mechanically interchangeable.

Move Pool Design Rules

Affinity Access

A creature may learn techniques from: - Its own Affinity or Affinities - The General Move Pool - Other Affinity pools where species compatibility is explicitly established Possessing an Affinity does not automatically grant every move within that Affinity.

Anatomy

Species anatomy determines compatibility with physical techniques. A wingless creature should not automatically learn wing-based techniques. A creature without suitable limbs, fangs, horns, claws, etc. should not automatically learn techniques that require those anatomical features.

Evolution

Evolution may unlock additional techniques while retaining previously learned compatible moves. A change in Affinity may expand or alter the available move pool.

Signature Moves

Species-specific Signature Moves are separate from this global database. Signature Moves should reinforce the creature’s identity rather than simply duplicate an existing generic move.

Balance

Move Power, Accuracy and PP should be considered together. High-power techniques generally have one or more balancing factors such as: - Lower Accuracy - Low PP - Recoil - Stat reduction - Preparation requirements - Conditional availability - Self-debuffs - Strong defensive counterplay

Species Learnsets

The global move pool is a source catalogue. An individual species should receive a small curated learnset, not the entire pool. Its learnset should reflect: - Affinity - Anatomy - Species concept - Evolution stage - Combat identity - Rarity - Personality or lore where appropriate
